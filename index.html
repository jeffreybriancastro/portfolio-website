Six workflows, one booking pipeline

Built in GoHighLevel. Published and running.

Every stage of a booking has a workflow behind it. An enquiry from Messenger or the site form becomes an opportunity with an owner on it; from there the pipeline moves itself through booked, attended, no-show, repeat or lost, and each move fires the follow-up that stage needs.

Enquiry in

Messenger reply or site form

Booked

Confirmed, then reminded twice

Attended

Thanked, stage moves itself

Repeat client

Promo a week later

No show

Chased for a rebooking

Closed lost

Three emails over two months

  1. 001

    New enquiry intake

    A Messenger reply or a form submission tags the contact, finds or creates the opportunity, assigns an owner and notifies the team. An hour later it checks whether a booking happened and flags the ones that did not.

  2. 002

    Appointment booked

    Confirmation email the moment the slot is taken, a reminder an hour later, and a final one after that.

  3. 003

    Attended

    The team gets the notification, the client gets a thank-you, and the deal moves itself to Closed / Repeat.

  4. 004

    No show

    A no-show email goes out, then the workflow checks for a rebooking after an hour and again two days later. Two days after that with still no booking, a last email goes out and the deal moves to Closed Lost.

  5. 005

    Closed / repeat

    Tagged as a repeat client, then a promo email seven days later.

  6. 006

    Closed lost

    The no-show tag comes off and three re-engagement emails go out at two weeks, then twenty days later, then thirty after that.

What do you actually build?

Four things, and most clients start with one: automation workflows, a GoHighLevel CRM setup, a sales funnel, or a website. They tend to end up connected, because a funnel that does not feed the CRM is just a page.

Do I need to already be on GoHighLevel?

No. If you are, I build inside the account you have. If you are not, I can set it up, or wire what you do use through n8n, Zapier or Make instead. The point is that it runs on your stack, not that you move to mine.
